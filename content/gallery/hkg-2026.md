Hong Kong is full of these markets, I've stumbled into them both in Kowloon as well as Hong Kong Island. I wish I had known about them before, I didn't make time to go visit them when they were at their busiest. Cramped shops with bright lighting—often shades of red or blue that really caught my eye. The two big markets I ended up visiting were in Sai Ying Pun in Hong Kong Island and near Mong Kok outside the South Station.

## Happy Valley

Happy Wednesdays happen nearly every week at the Happy Valley Racetrack in the aptly named neighborhood of Happy Valley. Foreigners get in for free if you show your passport, otherwise it's a 10 HKD fee. This was one of the few things I had planned before showing up to Hong Kong, and I'm happy I went. The horse betting culture runs deep in Hong Kong, you'll find Jockey Club storefronts scattered throughout different neighborhoods.

## New Territories

40 minutes by MTR from Admiralty lies Yuen Long, in the New Territories. It lies near the Chinese Border, and is home to the [Rooster Ridge Hike](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/hong-kong/tai-po/rooster-ridge-hike) in Lam Tsuen Country Park. We did this hike at the end of March, and the heat and humidity was already settling in.

As we walk towards the Country Park, we pass a small village on the outskirts. The short buildings, even small houses, are a big departure from the highrises on Hong Kong Island and Kowloon. It's my first time experiencing the mountainous landscape in Hong Kong. In hindsight, most of Hong Kong is like this-- undeveloped green space. Up until now, I never ventured past Kowloon, it was fascinating to see this side of the region.

## Star Ferry

Traveling between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central/Wan Chai, the Star Ferry's an icon of Victoria Harbour. Fares for adults range from $4.0 HKD to $6.5 HKD depending on whether you're traveling during the weekends and which deck (upper or lower) you choose. That's between 50 cents and 83 cents.

## Mong Kok

I kept coming back to this place, the high rises, the neon signs, the good food, and Sim City. If you're a photographer you should check this place out. They have all sorts of new and used camera equipment there, including the elusive X100VI. Be prepared to haggle, most of the items I saw and wanted usually had no prices on them, so be ready to look things up if you want to spend some money.
